How to read the map

Four questions decide where everyone sits

Left ↔ right · X axis

What is fundamental?

If you broke everything down to its most basic ingredient, would you find mind or matter?

Think of a Lego castle. The bricks are fundamental, and the castle is built from them. This axis asks what the universe's bricks are. Is awareness built out of atoms, or do atoms exist in, and depend on, a mind?

  1. −100Only mindPhysical things are ideas or appearances in a mind.Berkeley, Śaṅkara
  2. −50Mind firstMatter is real, but a mind made it or holds it in being.Augustine, Aquinas
  3. 0BothMind and matter are both basic, or two sides of one deeper thing.Spinoza, Chalmers
  4. +50Matter firstMind is real, but it comes from matter, or spirit is a finer kind of matter.Joseph Smith, Hobbes
  5. +100Only matterMinds are simply what brains do.Democritus, Dennett

Quick check: could a mind exist with no matter at all? If yes, you lean left. Could matter exist with no mind anywhere? If yes, you lean right.

Up ↕ down · Y axis

Where is the ultimate?

If you keep asking "but why?" about everything, where does the chain finally stop?

The ultimate is that last answer: the thing that explains everything else and needs nothing to explain it. Like a child asking "why?" over and over, this axis asks where the questions run out.

  1. +100Wholly beyondThe final answer is outside the universe, the way a painter is outside a painting.Plotinus, Islam
  2. +50Beyond and presentThe source is beyond the universe and also runs through all of it.Sikhism, Laozi
  3. 0The world itselfThe universe is the final answer, sacred or rational in its own right.Spinoza, the Stoics
  4. −50Only this worldThere is no final answer. The universe just exists.Hume, Marx
  5. −100ConstructedThe question is a mistake. "Why" only works inside human language and culture.Foucault, Derrida

Quick check: is there something more real than the universe that the universe depends on? If yes, you sit near the top.

Front ↔ back · Z axis

Is truth found or made?

Was 2 + 2 = 4 true before anyone counted? Could anyone, even God, make cruelty good just by approving of it?

Some truths look like discoveries: they were there before us, the way a mountain is there before the climber. Others look like inventions, like the rules of chess. This axis asks which kind the deepest truths are. It is the oldest question in philosophy (Plato's Euthyphro asked whether the gods love the good because it is good, or whether it is good because they love it), and it is where traditions that agree about God often split.

  1. +100EternalUncreated truths that bind every mind, even God's.Plato, Latter-day Saints, Jainism
  2. +50NecessaryFixed, because they flow from a nature no one chose.Aquinas, Leibniz, Catholicism
  3. 0GivenObjective for us, but they could have been otherwise.Dennett, Quine
  4. −50WilledSet by a will or imposed by the mind.Ockham, Descartes, William James
  5. −100Made by usCreated by culture, language or choice.Nietzsche, Sartre, Foucault

Who makes it? "Made" means very different things at the top and bottom of the map. Ockham and Nietzsche both sit at the back, but Ockham's truth is willed by God (high on Y) and Nietzsche's is willed by us (low on Y). Turn the model to the SIDE view to see this.

Fourth question · shown by color

What is the highest good?

If you had to say what a life is for, what would you say?

This is where a philosophy or religion says what it is arguing for. It isn't a scale with two ends, so it's shown as a category and a color. Two systems can disagree about everything on the three axes and still aim at the same thing: the Stoics and the Buddhists are far apart on where the ultimate is, but both hold that the good life is freedom from craving and disturbance, and both train for it. Plato, Augustine and Latter-day Saint teaching all say the end of life is becoming like God. Nietzsche, who sits at the opposite corner of the model from Plato, also aims at the opposite good: making your own values rather than ascending to a Good already there.

  1. Union with the divineBecoming like God, seeing God, or being joined to the One: the soul's ascent, the beatific vision, exaltation, union with Brahman.Plato, Augustine, Latter-day Saints, Śaṅkara
  2. Character and flourishingExcellence of character and a full, well-lived human life (eudaimonia): wisdom, courage, justice, humaneness, practiced in a community.Aristotle, Kant, Confucianism
  3. Liberation and peaceFreedom from craving, passion and disturbance: the untroubled mind, release from the cycle, the sage who cannot be shaken.The Stoics, Epicurus, Buddhism, Jainism
  4. Happiness and wellbeingPleasure, satisfied desire and the relief of suffering, for yourself and for everyone your life touches.Hume, Locke, Russell
  5. Freedom and self-creationAutonomy and authenticity: making your own values, overcoming what you were handed, being fully your own.Nietzsche, Sartre, Hegel
  6. Justice and the common goodA just, free society in which people flourish together; repairing the world.Marx, Derrida
  7. Harmony with the way of thingsLiving in accord with nature, the Dao, the kami, the land and the ancestors: balance, purity, reciprocity.Laozi, Shinto, Animism
  8. UnderstandingKnowledge and contemplation of what is: the life of reason, the vision of truth, understanding as the highest activity.Parmenides, Spinoza, Husserl

See it on the map: points are colored by their highest good; tap a good under the model to show only the entries that aim at it. Your test matches count this question alongside the three axes, and your results show who aims at the same good you do.

Why separate questions? They're independent. Catholics and Latter-day Saints both put the ultimate at the top, but split left and right over whether God is pure spirit or has a glorified body. The Stoics and Dennett both sit on the matter side, but split top and bottom over whether the universe is sacred or simply there. Latter-day Saints and Plato split on matter, yet sit together at the front of the model, where intelligence and law are uncreated and even God works within them; most Christian theology sits further back, with truth grounded in God's own nature. Plato and the Stoics agree that truth is found, yet aim at different goods: likeness to God for one, the untroubled sage for the other.

Drafting notes. Coordinates are interpretive judgments, not measurements. "Supported" positions follow the strongest scholarly reading of what each system requires, which sometimes differs from what the author said about it. Where the two agree, only one point shows. Region borders sit exactly halfway between anchor values (X −75, −25, +25; Y +25, −25, −75; Z 0), so each region's size follows the axes rather than a school. Every column except Anti-Realism splits at Z 0 into a truth-found half (solid) and a truth-made half (dashed). Who makes truth is read off the vertical axis: God's will sits high, human construction sits low. Classical theism sits left of center because its ultimate ground, God, is an immaterial mind.
