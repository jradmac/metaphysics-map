Y-AXISWhere the ultimate is

The ultimate is the deepest reality: what everything else depends on, which depends on nothing further. Theism says it is God, pantheism says it is the universe itself, and physicalism says there is no such thing.

+100 wholly beyond the world · +50 beyond it and also present in it · 0 the ultimate is the world itself · −50 only this world, nothing ultimate · −100 even "the world" is constructed, or the question is rejected.

DRAFTING NOTES

Coordinates are interpretive judgments, not measurements. "Supported" positions follow the strongest scholarly reading of what each system requires, which sometimes differs from what the author said about it. Where the two agree, only one point shows. Region borders sit exactly halfway between anchor values (X −75, −25, +25; Y +25, −25, −75), so each region's size follows the axes rather than a school. Classical theism sits left of center because its ultimate ground, God, is an immaterial mind.

How to read the map

Three questions decide where everyone sits

Left ↔ right · X axis

What is fundamental?

If you broke everything down to its most basic ingredient, would you find mind or matter?

Think of a Lego castle. The bricks are fundamental, and the castle is built from them. This axis asks what the universe's bricks are. Is awareness built out of atoms, or do atoms exist in, and depend on, a mind?

  1. −100Only mindPhysical things are ideas or appearances in a mind.Berkeley, Śaṅkara
  2. −50Mind firstMatter is real, but a mind made it or holds it in being.Augustine, Aquinas
  3. 0BothMind and matter are both basic, or two sides of one deeper thing.Spinoza, Chalmers
  4. +50Matter firstMind is real, but it comes from matter, or spirit is a finer kind of matter.Joseph Smith, Hobbes
  5. +100Only matterMinds are simply what brains do.Democritus, Dennett

Quick check: could a mind exist with no matter at all? If yes, you lean left. Could matter exist with no mind anywhere? If yes, you lean right.

Up ↕ down · Y axis

Where is the ultimate?

If you keep asking "but why?" about everything, where does the chain finally stop?

The ultimate is that last answer: the thing that explains everything else and needs nothing to explain it. Like a child asking "why?" over and over, this axis asks where the questions run out.

  1. +100Wholly beyondThe final answer is outside the universe, the way a painter is outside a painting.Plotinus, Islam
  2. +50Beyond and presentThe source is beyond the universe and also runs through all of it.Sikhism, Laozi
  3. 0The world itselfThe universe is the final answer, sacred or rational in its own right.Spinoza, the Stoics
  4. −50Only this worldThere is no final answer. The universe just exists.Hume, Marx
  5. −100ConstructedThe question is a mistake. "Why" only works inside human language and culture.Foucault, Derrida

Quick check: is there something more real than the universe that the universe depends on? If yes, you sit near the top.

Third question · shown by color

Is the ultimate a someone or a something?

Does the deepest reality know you? When you pray, is anyone listening?

Two views can share a spot on the map and still feel nothing alike. Plato and Judaism both place an immaterial ultimate beyond the world. But Plato's Good is a something you reason your way toward, while the God of the Hebrew Bible is a someone who speaks, commands and makes covenants.

  1. −100A somethingImpersonal, or nothing ultimate at all.Spinoza, the Dao, Dennett
  2. −50Mostly impersonalOrdered or mind-like, but it doesn't know you.Plato's Good, Aristotle's mover
  3. 0MixedSelf-aware or providential, but not a person beside us.Hegel, the Stoics
  4. +50Partly personalSpirits, or a companion God who responds.Shinto, Whitehead
  5. +100A someoneKnows you, speaks and loves.Judaism, Latter-day Saints

See it on the map: choose "Personal ↔ impersonal" under "Color points by". Your test matches count this question too.

Why separate questions? They're independent. Catholics and Latter-day Saints both put the ultimate at the top, but split left and right over whether God is pure spirit or has a glorified body. The Stoics and Dennett both sit on the matter side, but split top and bottom over whether the universe is sacred or simply there. Plato and Judaism sit side by side on the map, yet at opposite ends of the third question.
