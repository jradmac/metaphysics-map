# Metaphysical Field Survey

A CAD-style map of 46 philosophers and 15 religious traditions, placed by their metaphysical claims, plus a test that puts your own pins on the map.

## The two axes

- **What is fundamental?** (left ↔ right): if you broke everything down to its most basic ingredient, would you find mind or matter?
- **Where is the ultimate?** (up ↕ down): if you keep asking "but why?", where does the chain finally stop: beyond the universe, in the universe itself, or nowhere at all?

## What's on the map

- **Two points per thinker:** a solid point for what they claimed, and a hollow point for where their arguments actually commit them, joined by a drift arrow. Contested readings are dashed.
- **Nine regions** named after their schools (Idealism, Classical Theism, Materialist Theism, Pantheism, Physicalism and so on), each with an explanation.
- **Profiles** for every entry: a definition, its position, major works or key texts, and a link to read more.
- **Axis lines:** select anyone to see who shares their view on one axis, even when they're far apart on the other.

## The test

Twenty-six questions in five parts. It opens in its own tab, and when you finish, it closes and places your pins on the map.

1. **How you decide** comes first, before any beliefs are on your mind. Six real-life scenarios, including the hardest decision you made this past year. Pick up to three answers each and star the one that matters most. This places your **decision pin**.
2. **Your mind, The universe, Meaning and Truth** place your **belief pin**. They ask about consciousness, the soul, why there is anything at all, what happens at death, and whether moral truths are real.

Your results show your region (with a note if you sit near a border), how consistent your answers were, your closest philosopher and religion, your decision mix, whose way of deciding is closest to yours, and whether your decisions follow your beliefs.

### How the test was checked

Each question was answered as 57 of the mapped thinkers and traditions would answer it, based on their own writings, and the results were compared with their places on the map. The current version puts 45 of 57 in the correct region, with an average error of about 19 units. 27 of 32 thinkers come out with decisions aligned with their own metaphysics.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in any browser. It's one self-contained file with no build step, and it works offline except for the web font.

## A note on accuracy

Coordinates are interpretive judgments grounded in each thinker's own writing and the main scholarly readings, not measurements. Where serious scholars disagree, the map says so.
